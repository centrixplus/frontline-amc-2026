NDA Redline & Counter-Proposal

Original NDA Date: 23rd April 2025
Prepared by: Centrix Plus Integrated Solution – FZCO
Prepared on: 20th May 2026
Status: Proposed Amendments – Pending Frontline Review

Centrix Plus Integrated Solution – FZCO ("Vendor") has reviewed the draft Non-Disclosure Agreement provided by Frontline Express Company ("Company") and is fully committed to protecting all confidential, operational, and customer data of the Company. To enable a balanced and legally sound execution of this Agreement, the Vendor respectfully proposes the following amendments. These amendments do not weaken the Company's data protection rights — they clarify scope, align the NDA with the parallel AMC Agreement, and bring the document in line with standard international vendor NDA practice.

Detailed Redline by Section

Amendment 1 — Section 2 (Definition of Confidential Information)

Issue: The current definition has no exclusions. As written, even publicly available information or information the Vendor already possessed could be deemed confidential.

Add the following sub-section at the end of Section 2:

Exclusions from Confidential Information. Notwithstanding the foregoing, Confidential Information shall not include information that: (a) is or becomes publicly available through no breach of this Agreement by the Vendor; (b) was already lawfully known to the Vendor prior to disclosure by the Company; (c) is independently developed by the Vendor without reference to the Confidential Information; (d) is lawfully received by the Vendor from a third party without a duty of confidentiality; or (e) is required to be disclosed by law, court order, or competent regulatory authority, provided the Vendor gives the Company prompt prior written notice to allow the Company to seek a protective order.

Rationale: This is standard in every commercial NDA worldwide and protects the Vendor from being held liable for disclosing genuinely public information.

Amendment 2 — Section 4 (Production Environment Change Control)

Issue: In active security incidents or outages, waiting for written approval can worsen the incident.

Amend the "Production Environment Change Control" bullet as follows:

Production Environment Change Control: Make no changes to production systems, live databases, DMS workflows, server configurations, routing logic, IVR flows, APIs, integrations, security controls, or operational settings without prior written approval from authorized Company personnel; provided, however, that in the event of an active security incident, system outage, or critical service disruption requiring immediate action to restore service or contain harm, the Vendor may take necessary emergency measures and shall notify the Company as soon as reasonably practicable thereafter, with a full written report submitted within twenty-four (24) hours.

Rationale: Aligns with industry best practice for incident response (NIST, ISO 27035) and protects the Company's uptime, not the opposite.

Amendment 3 — Section 5 (No Disruption to Operations) [HIGH PRIORITY]

Issue: Combined with Section 10 liquidated damages, any unplanned downtime — even from a failed Microsoft patch or hardware failure — could be argued as a breach.

Amend the "No Disruption to Operations" bullet as follows:

No Disruption to Operations: The Vendor shall ensure that support activities, maintenance work, upgrades, patches, technical changes, or troubleshooting activities do not intentionally disrupt delivery operations, call center activities, customer services, production systems, or banking operations. The following shall not constitute a disruption or breach under this clause: (a) scheduled maintenance windows agreed in writing with the Company; (b) downtime caused by third-party service providers, hardware failures, telecommunications outages, or upstream vendors outside the Vendor's reasonable control; (c) events of Force Majeure; and (d) outages caused by changes implemented by Company personnel or other third parties not under the Vendor's direction. Service availability targets are governed exclusively by the parallel AMC Agreement between the parties.

Rationale: Without this carve-out, the NDA effectively imposes 100% uptime liability — which contradicts the 98% uptime target already agreed in the AMC.

Amendment 4 — Section 6 (Data Ownership) [HIGH PRIORITY]

Issue: As written, "business information processed through systems deployed by the Vendor" could be read to include the Vendor's own proprietary software, configurations, and IP.

Add the following sub-section at the end of Section 6:

Vendor Intellectual Property Carve-Out. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Vendor retains all rights, title, and ownership of its pre-existing and independently developed intellectual property, including but not limited to its proprietary software, source code, object code, tools, methodologies, frameworks, scripts, configurations, libraries, know-how, and any modifications, improvements, or derivative works thereof. The Company acquires only the right to use such Vendor IP solely for the purpose of operating the deployed systems for the duration and under the terms of the parallel AMC Agreement. Nothing in this Agreement shall transfer, assign, or license any Vendor IP to the Company beyond such operational use rights.

Rationale: Mirrors the IP clause already in the AMC Agreement (Section 14) and prevents inadvertent transfer of Centrix Plus's proprietary platforms (DMS, PBX integrations, etc.).

Amendment 5 — Section 7 (Term) [HIGH PRIORITY]

Issue: 5-year survival is excessive for general operational data. International standard is 2–3 years.

Amend Section 7 as follows:

"Obligations concerning confidentiality shall survive for a period of Five (5) years following the date of last access or termination of the visit/services, whichever is later."
Obligations concerning confidentiality shall survive for a period of Three (3) years following the date of last access or termination of the visit/services, whichever is later; provided that obligations relating to information that constitutes a "trade secret" under applicable law shall survive for so long as such information retains its status as a trade secret.

Rationale: Brings the term in line with international vendor NDA norms while keeping perpetual protection for true trade secrets.

Amendment 6 — Section 8 (Return of Materials)

Issue: Vendor is required by Kuwait commercial law, UAE FZCO regulations, and tax authorities to retain certain records.

Amend Section 8 by adding the following sentence:

Notwithstanding the above, the Vendor may retain (i) copies of Confidential Information to the extent required by applicable law, regulation, audit, or tax obligations; (ii) automated backups created in the ordinary course of business which cannot be feasibly deleted, provided such backups remain subject to the confidentiality obligations of this Agreement until securely overwritten; and (iii) one archival copy held by Vendor's legal counsel solely for purposes of dispute resolution or defense of legal claims.

Rationale: Standard legal-hold and regulatory-retention carve-out present in virtually all enterprise NDAs.

Amendment 7 — Section 10 (Remedies) [HIGH PRIORITY]

Issue: Open-ended liquidated damages with no cap and no defined amount create unlimited and unquantifiable exposure for the Vendor, disproportionate to the AMC contract value.

Replace Section 10 in its entirety with:

10. Remedies and Limitation of Liability. The Vendor acknowledges that any unauthorized disclosure, data leakage, or material breach of security protocols may result in harm to the Company. In such event, the Company shall be entitled to seek injunctive relief and compensatory damages for direct, proven losses.

Limitation of Liability. Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement, the Vendor's aggregate liability under or in connection with this Agreement shall not exceed the total fees actually paid by the Company to the Vendor under the parallel AMC Agreement during the twelve (12) months immediately preceding the event giving rise to the claim.

Exclusion of Indirect Damages. In no event shall either party be liable to the other for any indirect, incidental, consequential, special, punitive, or exemplary damages, loss of profits, loss of revenue, loss of business opportunity, or loss of goodwill, even if advised of the possibility of such damages.

Liquidated Damages. Any liquidated damages payable under this Agreement shall be (i) limited to documented and verified direct losses; (ii) subject to the overall liability cap set out above; and (iii) agreed in writing between the parties on a case-by-case basis, as no pre-agreed liquidated damages amount is established by this Agreement.

Carve-Outs. The above limitations shall not apply to (a) the Vendor's intentional or fraudulent breach of confidentiality obligations; (b) gross negligence; or (c) liabilities that cannot be excluded under applicable Kuwait law.

Rationale: Brings the remedies clause in line with standard enterprise vendor agreements. The carve-outs ensure the Company retains full recourse for the worst-case scenarios (fraud, gross negligence) while protecting the Vendor from disproportionate claims for minor or contributory issues.

Amendment 8 — Section 11 (Governing Law)

Issue: Vendor is a UAE FZCO entity. Pure Kuwait-court jurisdiction creates enforcement and cost issues.

Amend Section 11 by adding the following:

This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of Kuwait. Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement that cannot be resolved through good-faith negotiation within thirty (30) days shall be finally settled by arbitration administered by the Kuwait Commercial Arbitration Centre (KCAC) under its applicable rules, by a sole arbitrator, conducted in the English language, with the seat of arbitration in Kuwait City. The award of the arbitrator shall be final and binding on the parties.

Rationale: Arbitration is faster, confidential (appropriate for an NDA dispute), and enforceable across both Kuwait and the UAE under the New York Convention.

Amendment 9 — Section 12 (Entire Agreement) [HIGH PRIORITY]

Issue: As written, this NDA could be argued to supersede the AMC Agreement signed between the parties.

Amend Section 12 as follows:

This Agreement represents the entire understanding between the parties with respect to the subject matter of confidentiality and supersedes all prior discussions, agreements, or understandings relating to the subject matter herein confidentiality only.

Relationship to AMC Agreement. This Non-Disclosure Agreement supplements, and does not replace, modify, or supersede, the Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC) Agreement between the parties. In the event of any conflict between the terms of this NDA and the AMC Agreement, the terms of the AMC Agreement shall prevail with respect to scope of services, commercial terms, service levels, liability limitations, and intellectual property; and the terms of this NDA shall prevail solely with respect to confidentiality obligations.

Rationale: Prevents the NDA from accidentally overriding the carefully-negotiated AMC commercial terms (uptime, fees, scope exclusions).

Amendment 10 — New Section 13 (Permitted Disclosures)

Insert a new Section 13:

13. Permitted Disclosures. The Vendor may disclose Confidential Information to its directors, officers, employees, subcontractors, professional advisors, and contracted support personnel ("Representatives") strictly on a need-to-know basis for the performance of services under the AMC Agreement, provided that such Representatives are bound by confidentiality obligations no less protective than those contained in this Agreement. The Vendor shall remain responsible for any breach of this Agreement by its Representatives.

Rationale: Without this clause, the Vendor cannot even share Company-related information internally with its own engineers — making it impossible to deliver the AMC services.

Amendment 11 — New Section 14 (Notices)

Insert a new Section 14:

14. Notices and Communications. Any notice, consent, approval, or written communication required under this Agreement shall be in writing and shall be delivered by email to the designated authorized representatives of each party, with confirmation of receipt. Each party shall notify the other in writing of its designated representative and any changes thereto. Email communications from a party's designated representative shall constitute valid written notice under this Agreement.

Rationale: Eliminates ambiguity about how approvals (required throughout Sections 3, 4, 5) are validly given.

Sections Accepted Without Modification

Centrix Plus accepts the following sections of the original NDA in their entirety, without any proposed changes:

  • Section 1 — Purpose
  • Section 2 — Definition of Confidential Information (subject to the addition of the Exclusions sub-section above)
  • Section 3 — General Obligations of the Vendor (including Call Recording Protection, Data Handling & Backup Restrictions, and Live Data Usage Restriction)
  • Section 4 — All other Technical & Operational Security Controls (Rider Mobile App Security, Infrastructure Access Restrictions, VPN & Remote Access Security, User Account & Access Management, API & Integration Security, Access Revocation Upon Employee Exit, Remote Support Monitoring)
  • Section 5 — Operational Downtime & Incident Notification, Incident Investigation Cooperation
  • Section 6 — Data Ownership of customer, banking, shipment, delivery, call recording, and operational data remaining with the Company (subject to Vendor IP carve-out)
  • Section 6 — Public Disclosure Restriction
  • Section 9 — No License or Ownership

Closing Statement

Centrix Plus Integrated Solution – FZCO appreciates Frontline Express Company's diligence in protecting its operational and customer data, and stands fully behind the spirit and intent of this NDA. The proposed amendments are technical and legal refinements only — they do not reduce the Company's protection over its data; they make the Agreement balanced, executable in good faith, and consistent with the parallel AMC Agreement already under discussion.

We look forward to Frontline's feedback and to signing a final, mutually agreed version at the earliest opportunity.
